# Nature Reviews guidance

Source: [bundled artwork guide](natrev-artworkguide.pdf). Physical page numbers count the opening artwork table as page 1; the following four pages are printed as pages 1-4. This is distilled guidance; consult the PDF for exact wording and visual examples.

## General principles

- **Physical page 1:** Complement the narrative, use logical flow, remove unnecessary detail, label clearly, keep encodings consistent, group long lists, and use imagery only when it helps understanding.
- **Physical page 2 / printed page 1:** Match visual hierarchy to scientific importance. Saturated focus elements, quieter context, and selective detail guide attention. Examples show a biological mechanism, object-detail levels, and the publisher palette.
- **Physical page 3 / printed page 2:** Visual editing is substantive redesign. Ask what is essential, missing, removable, repetitive, or decorative. Define objects and arrows; use panel labels and headings. Accessibility examples support contrast, black type, suitable text backgrounds, and checks for colour blindness.
- **Physical page 4 / printed page 3:** Use colour for hierarchy, categories, scientific conventions, and appropriate natural appearance. Examples distinguish accent colours, realistic environments, discipline conventions, and complex grouped information.
- **Physical page 5 / printed page 4:** Processes or phenomena suit conceptual figures; categorized ideas may work better as a table/list. Avoid crowding and give panels and labels adequate space.

## Publisher-specific statements

These describe the supplied guide's context, not universal defaults or guaranteed current policy for every Nature journal.

| Topic | Source guidance |
| --- | --- |
| Drafts and lifecycle | Physical page 1 allows common drawing software, PowerPoint, reference images, and some hand sketches. Provide scientific detail for peer review. Art Editors redraw/redesign accepted-in-principle figures, including BioRender drafts. Physical page 4 notes third-party artwork is edited too. |
| Panels and lists | Physical page 1 suggests probably no more than six panels for a full-page figure and approximately five points per list. These are recommendations, not hard limits. |
| Typography | Physical page 1 describes bold headings and 8 pt house-style text, discouraging italics or font-size variation for hierarchy. Physical page 5 requests at least 7 pt text in draft figures. Keep draft and house-style guidance distinct. |
| Colour | Physical pages 1, 2, and 4 describe the Reviews/Nature-branded palette, contextual colours, and accents. Flag scientifically meaningful choices to the editor. Scientific conventions can justify off-palette colours. Do not invent exact hex values from screenshots. |
| Photographs | Physical page 1 prefers high-resolution unlabelled images, with TIFF at 300 dpi at final print size. Retain separate labels or supply unlabelled photographs. |
| Permissions | Physical page 1 prefers original figures; adaptation/reproduction may be possible. Retain source records for the editor to obtain permissions after peer review. The request not to obtain publisher permissions oneself applies to that editorial workflow; it neither authorizes reuse nor overrides other workflows. |
| Graphs | Physical page 1 calls for axes with units and colour/symbol keys. It discourages isolated-study data unless fundamental to the narrative, says original data are not published in that context, allows some editor-approved collations, and discourages publication-count trends. These restrictions do not govern agentic_srim research output generally. |
| Chemical structures | Physical page 1 prefers ChemDraw using the named chemical-structure guide and template; final files are .cdx. Verify current requirements when submitting. |
| Equations | Physical page 1 specifies italic variables, roman constants, and bold vectors for this style. |

Apply accessibility aims broadly, but evaluate actual encodings rather than assuming avoidance of one colour pair guarantees accessibility. The PDF chiefly concerns commissioned conceptual illustrations, including Reviews and News & Views; it is not a complete quantitative-chart standard. Read quantitative-charts.md for those checks. For submissions verify the requested journal's current instructions and flag conflicts with the historical source rather than silently choosing a rule.
